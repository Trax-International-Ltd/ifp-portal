Mountainous MOC Calculator

Sizes the additional clearance over mountainous terrain using the method of Pérez Sanz et al. (2024). The additional clearance is the wind-induced altimeter error (Eq. 1) plus the height lost in mountain-wave turbulence (Appendix A, by aircraft category and angle of attack). The design wind comes from segment altitude, so the increase grows with altitude. MOC is in metres; altitudes and elevations are in feet.

Lowest cat: the smallest aircraft category using the segment. The paper finds lower categories lose more height, so the lowest one governs. AOA: the paper's guidance is up to 4° en-route, −2° to 2° on arrival and up to 6° on departure or missed approach. Choosing a phase sets a default. Wind alt: leave blank to iterate on the resulting MOCA. Wind: leave blank for the ICAO standard wind (2h/1000 + 47 kt). The paper uses 30 kt for SID and final missed approach turns, which is the default for those phases.

Basis

V = 2h/1000 + 47 kt (Eq. 12)
Δhalt = −6.25×10⁻⁵V³ + 0.14V² − 0.825V + 14 ft (Eq. 1)
Δhturb = Table A1–A3 (cat, AOA, V) (App. A)
ΔMOC = min(Δhalt + Δhturb, cap × MOCbase)
MOCA = obstacle + (MOCbase + ΔMOC) ↑

The turbulence values come from the paper's gust-response chain (Eqs. 2–27). It runs: wind at 700 hPa (Hellman law) → mountain-wave class → vertical gust → load factor change Δn → derived gust velocity → height loss. It uses a reference aircraft per category at maximum PANS-OPS IAS and a low operating weight. The table below shows the paper's values for the 4° angle of attack in feet. Values between wind speeds are interpolated.
